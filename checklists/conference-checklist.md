# 🏛️ Conference Attendance Checklist

> Conferences are the lifeblood of CS research culture. Done well, a single conference can generate 3–5 meaningful collaborations, a job offer, and 6 months' worth of new research directions.

---

## Before the Conference (4–8 weeks out)

### Logistics

- [ ] Register for the conference early (student rates, early-bird discounts)
- [ ] Book accommodation — conference hotels fill fast; aim to stay where attendees stay
- [ ] Book flights (student travel grants often have specific booking requirements)
- [ ] Apply for student travel grants from the conference (see [career/grants-and-funding.md](../career/grants-and-funding.md))
- [ ] Apply for travel funding from your department/advisor
- [ ] Obtain a visa if required (allow 6–8 weeks for US B1/B2 or Schengen)
- [ ] Check if your institution needs a travel approval form

### Preparation

- [ ] Download the conference app or booklet
- [ ] Read through all accepted papers (at least titles and abstracts)
- [ ] Identify the **5 talks you must attend**
- [ ] Identify the **5 posters you must visit**
- [ ] Identify **5–10 researchers you want to meet** — research their recent work
- [ ] Send meeting request emails to target researchers 2–3 weeks in advance
- [ ] Prepare your **30-second elevator pitch** for your own research
- [ ] Prepare your **3-minute pitch** for detailed conversations

### If You Are Presenting

- [ ] Final version of talk slides completed
- [ ] Talk timed to exactly the allocated time (15 min → 15 min, not 17 min)
- [ ] Rehearsed at least 3 times with an audience
- [ ] Backup: slides uploaded to Google Drive or cloud storage
- [ ] Poster printed (if presenting a poster):
  - [ ] Correct dimensions for the conference (check requirements!)
  - [ ] Printed at 300 DPI
  - [ ] QR code linking to paper/project page is working
  - [ ] Physical print collected and packed in a tube

---

## At the Conference

### Daily Ritual

**Morning:**
- [ ] Review today's schedule; confirm which sessions and posters to attend
- [ ] Identify who you might run into and what you want to discuss

**During Sessions:**
- [ ] Take notes on papers that intersect with your research
- [ ] Note down questions to ask at poster sessions
- [ ] Collect contact info of interesting speakers (Twitter/X, LinkedIn, email)

**Poster Sessions:**
- [ ] Visit targeted posters and engage in technical conversations
- [ ] Be a good visitor: read the poster before asking questions
- [ ] Take a photo of the poster (with presenter's permission) for later reference
- [ ] If you have a poster: arrive 10 minutes early; bring business cards or QR code handouts

**Evening Social Events:**
- [ ] Attend workshop dinners, receptions, and social hours — this is where collaborations form
- [ ] Introduce yourself genuinely; ask what people are working on
- [ ] Exchange contact information before conversations end

### Networking Protocol

When meeting a new researcher:
1. Introduce yourself: name, institution, advisor, and one-sentence research description
2. Ask what they are working on — be genuinely curious
3. Look for a connection between your work and theirs
4. If there's a match: "We should talk more about this — are you free for coffee?"
5. Exchange contact information

> [!TIP]
> The best networking is not transactional — it is genuine intellectual curiosity. Ask about their work before talking about yours.

### If You Are Giving a Talk

- [ ] Arrive 15–20 min early to test AV setup
- [ ] Bring your own clicker + backup HDMI/USB-C adapters
- [ ] Have slides in PDF format as a backup (in case your laptop fails)
- [ ] Position yourself near the front before your session
- [ ] After your talk: stay for Q&A and linger — interested researchers will approach you

---

## After the Conference (within 1 week)

### Follow-Up

- [ ] Send personalized follow-up emails to everyone you had meaningful conversations with
  - Reference something specific you discussed
  - Mention next steps if you discussed collaboration
  - Connect on LinkedIn / follow on Twitter
- [ ] Act on any specific commitments made ("I'll send you our paper", "Let's schedule a call")

### Knowledge Capture

- [ ] Write a brief trip report for your lab (2–3 paragraphs on what you learned and who you met)
- [ ] Read the papers you flagged as interesting
- [ ] Update your research direction based on what you learned

### Administrative

- [ ] Submit expense reports with all receipts (conference registration, flights, hotel, meals)
- [ ] Share travel grant acknowledgment materials if required by the grant

## Conference Packing List

### Technology

- [ ] Laptop + charger
- [ ] USB-C to HDMI adapter (for talks)
- [ ] USB-A to USB-C adapter
- [ ] Portable battery bank
- [ ] Conference poster (if presenting) — in a protective tube
- [ ] Clicker / laser pointer (if presenting)

### Professional

- [ ] Business cards OR a QR code card linking to your website
- [ ] Printed copies of your poster or paper (optional, but appreciated)
