# 🎓 PhD Milestones Checklist

> A PhD is a 4–7 year project with no external accountability structure. This checklist gives you that structure — a year-by-year roadmap from day one through defense.

---

## Year 1: Foundation

### Semester 1

**Academic:**
- [ ] Complete registration and course enrollment
- [ ] Attend all required orientation events
- [ ] Meet with department graduate advisor to plan coursework
- [ ] Attend all department colloquia and seminars
- [ ] Read 10 foundational papers in your target area

**Community:**
- [ ] Introduce yourself to all faculty in your research area
- [ ] Join the graduate student association
- [ ] Identify potential lab rotation advisors (if applicable)
- [ ] Attend one reading group or journal club

**Infrastructure:**
- [ ] Set up all academic accounts (GitHub Education, ORCID, Google Scholar)
- [ ] Configure development environment and HPC access
- [ ] Set up reference manager (Zotero)

### Semester 2

- [ ] Complete first lab rotation or research project
- [ ] Produce a literature review or survey memo on a specific topic
- [ ] Identify 2–3 potential research directions
- [ ] Present preliminary ideas to a faculty member
- [ ] Begin advisor search conversations (see [guides/02-choosing-advisor.md](../guides/02-choosing-advisor.md))

**By end of Year 1:**
- [ ] Primary advisor selected and lab join confirmed
- [ ] Funding and stipend source confirmed
- [ ] Coursework plan for years 2–3 established
- [ ] First rough research idea documented in writing

---

## Year 2: First Research Project

### Semester 3

- [ ] Begin first research project with advisor
- [ ] Meet with advisor weekly (at minimum biweekly)
- [ ] Set up experiment tracking system (W&B, MLflow)
- [ ] Read 30–50 papers in your specific research area
- [ ] Submit to at least one workshop or non-archival venue

### Semester 4

- [ ] Submit first paper to a conference (even if rejected — the process is the training)
- [ ] Begin qualifying exam preparation
- [ ] Identify potential committee members
- [ ] Attend first major conference in your area
- [ ] Update your personal website with your research

**By end of Year 2:**
- [ ] At least one paper under review or submitted
- [ ] Committee assembled (or plan in place)
- [ ] Clear primary research direction identified
- [ ] First conference attended
- [ ] Completed all required coursework (or on track)

---

## Year 3: Qualifying Exam & First Publication

### Qualifying Exam Milestones

- [ ] Confirm exam format with department (written, oral, proposal-based)
- [ ] Obtain list of past qualifying exam questions from department
- [ ] Form study group with peers
- [ ] Complete practice exams / mock oral exam
- [ ] **PASS QUALIFYING EXAM** ← Major milestone
- [ ] Celebrate appropriately

### Research Milestones

- [ ] First major conference paper accepted (A or A* venue)
- [ ] Present at the conference
- [ ] Dissertation proposal drafted (for proposal-based programs)
- [ ] **DISSERTATION PROPOSAL DEFENDED** (if required)

**By end of Year 3:**
- [ ] Qualifying exam passed
- [ ] First publication in a top-tier venue
- [ ] Dissertation scope defined
- [ ] Apply for NSF GRFP or equivalent fellowship (if eligible)

---

## Year 4: Research Momentum

### Publications

- [ ] Second major conference or journal paper submitted
- [ ] Consider a journal extension of your best conference paper
- [ ] Present at 2+ conferences or workshops this year

### Career Development

- [ ] Apply for research internship at industry lab (applications open August–November)
- [ ] Complete internship (summer) and negotiate return offer / paper plan
- [ ] Update CV and research statement
- [ ] Begin thinking about job market timeline

### Dissertation

- [ ] Write at least one dissertation chapter
- [ ] Schedule informal committee meeting to present progress
- [ ] Identify the core thesis: "My dissertation argues that..."

**By end of Year 4:**
- [ ] 2–3 publications in top venues
- [ ] Internship completed
- [ ] At least one dissertation chapter drafted
- [ ] Career path decision (academia vs. industry) solidified

---

## Year 5: Dissertation and Job Market

### First Half of Year 5

**Dissertation:**
- [ ] All dissertation chapters drafted
- [ ] Committee reads full draft
- [ ] Incorporate committee feedback
- [ ] Final committee approval of dissertation

**Job Market (Academic):**
- [ ] Applications assembled (CV, research statement, teaching statement, diversity statement)
- [ ] Reference letters requested (minimum 3 months in advance)
- [ ] Applications submitted (October–November)
- [ ] Phone/video interviews (November–February)
- [ ] Campus visits (January–March)

**Job Market (Industry):**
- [ ] CV and resume updated
- [ ] LeetCode practice (if targeting engineering roles)
- [ ] Applications and referrals submitted
- [ ] Interviews completed

### Second Half of Year 5

- [ ] Job offer received and negotiated
- [ ] **DISSERTATION DEFENSE SCHEDULED**
- [ ] Final dissertation submitted to committee
- [ ] **DISSERTATION DEFENDED** 🎉
- [ ] Required revisions completed
- [ ] Final dissertation submitted to graduate school
- [ ] Degree conferred

---

## Recurring Annual Milestones

Every year of your PhD, repeat these:

### Academic Year Start (September)

- [ ] Renew GitHub Student Pack and other academic benefits
- [ ] Update personal website with new publications and projects
- [ ] Set annual research goals with advisor
- [ ] Update committee on progress (if not done at end of previous year)

### Conference Submission Seasons

- [ ] Monitor deadlines for your target venues
- [ ] Prepare papers 4–6 weeks before deadline
- [ ] Use [paper-submission-checklist.md](./paper-submission-checklist.md) before every submission

### Grant/Fellowship Applications

- [ ] NSF GRFP (October) — Year 1 or 2 of PhD
- [ ] Conference travel grants — before each conference
- [ ] Department fellowship competitions — typically September or January

---

## Wellbeing Milestones

These are equally important:

- [ ] Schedule a real vacation (not a "working vacation") once per year
- [ ] Maintain a non-research hobby or activity throughout the PhD
- [ ] Regularly assess: am I thriving, surviving, or struggling? Seek help early if struggling
- [ ] Annual reflection: Am I on track for my goals? Am I happy in this environment?
