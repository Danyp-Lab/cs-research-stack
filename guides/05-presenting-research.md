# 🎤 Presenting Your Research

> *"A great talk does not explain your paper — it makes people want to read it."*

Research communication does not end at the submission portal. Talks, posters, and demos at conferences are often how your work actually reaches the people who matter. A poorly presented excellent paper can be ignored; a compellingly presented good paper can define a field. This guide covers conference talks, poster sessions, and academic demos.

---

## 1. The Three Presentation Formats

### Conference Talk (15–30 min)

The formal oral presentation in a conference session. Audience: 50–500 researchers in your subfield. Goal: communicate your core insight and make the audience interested enough to read (or cite) your paper.

### Poster Session (60–120 min)

An informal one-on-one or small-group discussion at your printed poster. Audience: 5–50 researchers who walk by and stop. Goal: generate meaningful conversations, get feedback, and build relationships.

### Demo / Live System (variable)

A live or video demonstration of a working system. Audience: practitioners, industry researchers, potential collaborators. Goal: demonstrate real-world impact and feasibility.

## 2. Building a Conference Talk

### The Narrative Arc

A research talk is a story, not a lecture. Structure it as:

```
1. The Hook      — Why should anyone care? (1–2 min)
2. The Problem   — What specifically is broken? (2–3 min)
3. The Insight   — Your key idea in one sentence (1 min)
4. The Method    — How the insight becomes a system (5–10 min)
5. The Results   — Evidence that it works (3–5 min)
6. The Impact    — What changes because of this? (1–2 min)
```

> [!TIP]
> Open with a **concrete, surprising, or relatable example** — not with "In this paper, we present...". The audience needs a reason to care before they will listen to how.

### Slide Design Principles

| Principle | Bad Example | Good Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **One idea per slide** | 8 bullet points on one slide | One claim + one supporting figure |
| **Figures over text** | Dense paragraph explaining results | Clear graph with labeled axes |
| **Large fonts** | 14pt body text | 28pt minimum for body, 36pt for headings |
| **Consistent visual language** | Mixed font sizes, colors, styles | Unified color palette and typography |
| **Slide titles are claims** | "Results" | "Our method outperforms SOTA by 18% on..." |

### Slide Count Rules of Thumb

| Talk Length | Approximate Slide Count |
| :--- | :--- |
| 5 min (lightning) | 5–8 slides |
| 15 min (workshop) | 12–18 slides |
| 20 min (main conference) | 15–20 slides |
| 45 min (seminar) | 30–45 slides |

### Slide Tools

* **[Beamer (LaTeX)](https://ctan.org/pkg/beamer)** — Maximum consistency with paper content; steep learning curve. Use the Metropolis theme.
* **[PowerPoint](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/powerpoint)** — Widest compatibility; excellent animation support.
* **[Keynote](https://www.apple.com/keynote/)** — Superior default aesthetics; macOS only.
* **[Google Slides](https://slides.google.com/)** — Collaborative, browser-based; free.
* **[Canva](https://www.canva.com/)** — Beautiful templates; limited technical diagram support.

## 3. Delivering the Talk

### Rehearsal Protocol

| Stage | Method | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solo run-through** | Speak aloud, time yourself | Daily for the last week |
| **Lab rehearsal** | Present to labmates, collect questions | At least twice |
| **External rehearsal** | Present to someone outside your subfield | Once |
| **Tech check** | Test projector, clicker, font rendering | Day of |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Time yourself every rehearsal**. Running 5 minutes over is not an inconvenience — it means cutting Q&A and getting forcibly stopped mid-sentence.

### Managing Nervousness

- Accept that nervousness is normal and physiologically useful — it sharpens focus.
- The first 60 seconds are the hardest. Have your opening 3 sentences completely memorized.
- Speak slower than feels natural. Under stress, everyone speaks too fast.
- Make eye contact with friendly faces in the audience, especially early on.

### Handling Questions

- **Repeat the question** before answering — it ensures the whole room heard it, and buys you 3–5 seconds to think.
- **"That's a great question"** is filler. Skip it.
- For questions you cannot answer: *"I don't know — that's a great direction for future work. Could we discuss after the session?"*
- For hostile questions: stay calm, be factual, and never get defensive. *"That's a fair concern. What we show in Section 4 is..."*

## 4. Designing an Effective Poster

### Layout Architecture

A standard conference poster is typically **A0 size (841 × 1189mm)** or **36 × 48 inches**. Organize it into columns (3–4) that can be read top-to-bottom, left-to-right.

```
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
|     TITLE / AUTHORS / INSTITUTION / QR CODE                  |
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
|   Abstract /       |    Method          |   Results          |
|   Motivation       |    Overview        |   (Main Figure)    |
|                    |                    |                    |
|   Problem          |    Component A     |   Ablation         |
|   Statement        |    Component B     |                    |
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
|   Background       |    Implementation  |   Conclusion /     |
|                    |    Details         |   Future Work      |
+--------------------+--------------------+--------------------+
```

### Poster Design Rules

- **Large title** — readable from 3 meters.
- **Minimize text** — posters are a conversation starter, not a paper print-out.
- **Center your best figure** — the one image that summarizes your contribution.
- **QR code** — link to arXiv preprint, project page, or demo video.
- **Contact info** — name, email, and Twitter/X handle prominently displayed.

### Poster Tools

* **[Canva](https://www.canva.com/)** — Beautiful templates, easy to use, exports to PDF/PNG.
* **[Adobe Illustrator](https://www.adobe.com/products/illustrator.html)** — Professional quality; free via institution or Adobe Creative Cloud.
* **[Inkscape](https://inkscape.org/)** — Free and open-source vector editor. Excellent for technical diagrams.
* **[LaTeX with beamerposter](https://ctan.org/pkg/beamerposter)** — Full LaTeX control; useful when poster must match paper precisely.
* **[Figma](https://www.figma.com/)** — Collaborative, browser-based; great for design-heavy posters.

## 5. Conference Strategy

### Before the Conference

- [ ] Read the schedule and identify the 5 talks you must attend.
- [ ] Identify 3–5 researchers you want to meet and research their recent work.
- [ ] Prepare a **30-second pitch** of your own work.
- [ ] Schedule meetings via email before arrival — popular researchers fill up fast.

### At the Conference

- **Attend talks outside your subarea** — some of the best ideas come from adjacent fields.
- **Hang around after talks** — the most interesting conversations happen in the 10 minutes after a session ends.
- **Approach poster presenters actively** — ask genuine questions. Every researcher with a poster wants to talk about their work.
- **Attend the social events** — workshops, dinners, and parties are where collaborations actually form.

### After the Conference

- [ ] Follow up with everyone you met within 48 hours (email or LinkedIn connection).
- [ ] Note the papers you missed but want to read.
- [ ] Write a short trip report for your lab — it reinforces your own learning.

## 6. Building a Project Website

Every major paper benefits from a dedicated project page. This is now standard in ML/vision research.

### Project Page Template

A minimal, effective project page includes:
1. **Paper title and author list**
2. **Abstract**
3. **Teaser figure or video**
4. **Links**: Paper (arXiv/ACM/IEEE), Code (GitHub), Demo (Hugging Face Spaces / Colab)
5. **Main results** (key figures from the paper)
6. **BibTeX entry**

### Hosting Options

* **[GitHub Pages](https://pages.github.com/)** — Free, easy to deploy from a repository.
* **[Hugging Face Spaces](https://huggingface.co/spaces)** — Host live demos with Gradio or Streamlit. Free GPU tier available.
* **[Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/)** — Free CDN hosting with automatic deployment from GitHub.
