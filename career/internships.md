# 🏢 Research Internships

> A research internship at a top lab is one of the highest-ROI activities you can do during your PhD. It accelerates your publication record, expands your network, generates fellowship letters, and often leads directly to a job offer.

---

## 1. Why Internships Matter for PhD Students

- **Publication acceleration**: Industry labs have enormous compute and data. A 3-month internship can generate a paper that would take 12+ months in a typical academic setting.
- **Network building**: The researchers you work with become collaborators, letter writers, and referrers.
- **Career optionality**: A return offer from a top lab gives you leverage in both industry and academic job markets.
- **Compensation**: Summer internship stipends at major AI labs often reach $8,000–$15,000/month, which can be saved toward equipment or offset financial stress.
- **Perspective**: Working in industry sharpens your sense of what problems are impactful beyond academia.

## 3. The Application Timeline

Industry research internship applications follow a compressed and non-obvious timeline:

```
August–October    — Start networking at conferences (ICML, NeurIPS, CVPR)
September–November — Applications open for summer positions
October–December  — Primary application window (DO NOT MISS)
November–February — Interviews
January–March     — Offers released
June–September    — Internship takes place
```

> [!WARNING]
> Many students apply in February thinking they have time. By then, most positions are filled. The application window for summer internships often opens **9 months in advance**.

## 4. How to Get an Internship

### Path 1: Direct Application

Most labs have careers pages with intern listings. Apply directly and comprehensively:
- Tailor your cover letter / research statement to the lab's current projects.
- List specific researchers whose work aligns with yours.
- Include links to your best paper, preprint, or project.

### Path 2: Researcher Cold Outreach

More effective than direct applications for PhD students:

```
Subject: Research Internship Inquiry — [Your Research Area]

Dear Dr. [Last Name],

I am a PhD student at [University] working on [specific topic], 
advised by [Advisor Name]. My recent work on [specific result] 
was published at [Venue].

I noticed your work on [their specific paper/project], which closely 
aligns with my research interests. I would be very interested in 
exploring an internship opportunity in your group for Summer [Year].

I have attached my CV. You can find my publications at [Google Scholar link]
and my code at [GitHub link].

Thank you for your consideration.
[Name]
```

Keep it to 3–4 short paragraphs. Attach your CV. Do not attach your papers — link to them.

### Path 3: Conference Networking

The most natural pipeline: meet a researcher at a conference, have a genuine technical conversation about your work, and follow up about an internship. See [guides/05-presenting-research.md](../guides/05-presenting-research.md) for conference strategy.

## 5. The Research Internship Interview

Research internship interviews are different from software engineering interviews:

### What to Expect

| Stage | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Screening call** | Background, research interests, fit (30–45 min) |
| **Technical deep dive** | Present your best paper; questions on methods and evaluation |
| **Research problem discussion** | Novel problem-solving in your research area |
| **Coding (sometimes)** | Basic algorithms and data structures (less emphasis than SWE roles) |

### How to Prepare

1. **Know your own work deeply** — be ready to explain any choice in your paper.
2. **Know the interviewer's work** — read their 2–3 most recent papers before the call.
3. **Practice explaining your research** to a non-expert in 3 minutes.
4. **Prepare questions** — asking good questions signals genuine interest.

## 6. Maximizing Your Internship

### Week 1: Orientation
- Set up your development environment.
- Schedule 30-min coffees with 5–10 researchers in adjacent areas.
- Understand your project scope and deliverables.

### Weeks 2–10: Execution
- Set a clear, scoped goal that can produce a paper or significant result.
- Share weekly updates with your host — never let them be surprised.
- Attend all relevant seminars and reading groups.

### Final Weeks: Wrapping Up
- Prepare a final presentation for the lab.
- Discuss paper timeline and authorship with your host.
- Request a formal performance review.
- Ask about return offer / full-time process.
