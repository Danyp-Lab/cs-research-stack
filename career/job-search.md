# 💼 Job Search: Academia & Industry

> The academic and industry job markets run on entirely different timelines, norms, and evaluation criteria. This guide covers both tracks with the specificity required to navigate them effectively.

---

## 1. The Academic Faculty Job Market

### Timeline

The academic market runs on an annual cycle, driven by university hiring committees:

```
August        — Finalize application materials
September     — Jobs posted (check CRA, CS departments' websites)
October–November — Application deadline window
November–February — Phone / video interviews (20–30 min)
January–March — Campus visits ("fly-outs", 2-day process)
March–April   — Offers released; negotiation window
April 15      — Standard decision deadline (for US institutions)
```

### Where to Find Faculty Positions

* **[CRA Job Announcements](https://cra.org/ads/)** — The primary board for CS faculty positions.
* **[ACM Computing Job Listings](https://jobs.acm.org/)** — ACM-affiliated positions.
* **[CS Rankings Job Board](https://csrankings.org/)** — Some programs list openings here.
* **[HigherEdJobs](https://www.higheredjobs.com/)** — Broader academic positions.
* Direct department websites — Many positions are only advertised on department pages.

### Application Package Components

| Component | Length | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cover Letter** | 1–2 pages | Express specific fit and interest |
| **CV** | 3–10+ pages | Complete scholarly record |
| **Research Statement** | 2–5 pages | Past work + future vision |
| **Teaching Statement** | 1–2 pages | Philosophy and experience |
| **Diversity Statement** | 1–2 pages (increasingly required) | Commitment to inclusive environments |
| **3–5 Reference Letters** | Sent directly by referees | Expert external validation |
| **Writing Sample** | 1–2 representative papers | Evidence of publication quality |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Request recommendation letters **at least 3 months before deadlines**. Give your letter writers your complete materials, a clear statement of the positions you are applying to, and specific talking points about your work you would like them to address.

### The Campus Visit ("Fly-out")

If you receive a campus visit invitation, you are a serious finalist:

**Typical schedule:**
- Day 1: Faculty dinners, informal meetings with graduate students
- Day 2: Job talk (45–60 min), individual faculty meetings (6–10 x 30 min), department chair meeting

**Job talk structure:**
- 10 min: Motivation and vision
- 25 min: Your best 1–2 research projects (deep technical content)
- 10 min: Future research directions and funding plan
- 5 min: Teaching/service vision

## 2. Industry Research & Engineering Job Market

### Timeline

Industry hiring is continuous but has peak seasons:

```
September–November — Fall recruiting season (new-grad focused)
January–March     — Spring recruiting season
March–June        — PhD/research scientist hiring (often continuous)
```

### Job Categories and Salary Ranges

| Role | Level | Compensation Range (US, 2024) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Research Scientist** | Entry (PhD) | $180–280K total comp |
| **Research Scientist** | Senior | $280–450K+ |
| **Software Engineer** | L4/E4 (PhD equiv) | $200–350K |
| **ML Engineer** | Entry (PhD) | $200–320K |
| **Research Engineer** | Entry | $180–280K |

> Use [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/) for real, crowdsourced compensation data broken down by company, level, and location.

### Where to Find Industry Research Jobs

* **[Levels.fyi Job Board](https://www.levels.fyi/jobs)** — Compensation-transparent listings
* **[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/)** — Broadest reach; set up alerts
* **[Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/)** — Job listings + interview reviews
* **[Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/)** — Aggregated listings
* Company careers pages — Apply directly for best results
* **Conference recruiting**: NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR, and ICLR have massive recruiting events

### The Industry Interview Process

#### For Research Scientist Roles

1. **Recruiter screen** (30 min) — Background, compensation expectations
2. **Technical screen** (60 min) — Research presentation or coding
3. **Onsite / virtual loop** (4–6 hours):
   - Research presentation (45 min) — Present your best paper
   - Coding interview × 2 (LC easy/medium for research roles)
   - Research discussion × 2 — Novel problem solving
   - Behavioral interview × 1 — Leadership principles / values
4. **Offer and negotiation**

#### For Software Engineering Roles

1. **Online assessment** — 2–3 LeetCode-style problems
2. **Phone screen** (45 min coding)
3. **Virtual loop** (4–5 interviews): algorithms, system design, behavioral

### Technical Interview Preparation

**Coding (LeetCode):**
* **[NeetCode](https://neetcode.io/)** — Curated list of 150 essential problems with video solutions
* **[LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/)** — Practice platform; focus on arrays, trees, graphs, DP
* **[AlgoExpert](https://www.algoexpert.io/)** — Structured courses (paid but comprehensive)

**System Design:**
* **[System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer)** — Free comprehensive guide
* **[Designing Data-Intensive Applications](https://dataintensive.net/)** — The definitive book (Martin Kleppmann)

**ML System Design:**
* **[Machine Learning System Design Interview](https://www.amazon.com/Machine-Learning-System-Design-Interview/dp/1736049127)** — Alex Xu & Sahn Lam

## 3. Negotiation

Negotiation is expected. Not negotiating leaves significant compensation on the table.

### Principles

1. **Never give a number first** — "I'm focused on finding the right role; I'm confident we can agree on compensation."
2. **Compete offers create leverage** — Multiple offers dramatically improve your negotiation position.
3. **Everything is negotiable** — Base, equity, sign-on bonus, relocation, start date, title, team placement.
4. **Be enthusiastic, not desperate** — The best leverage comes from genuine alternatives.

### How to Ask for More

```
"Thank you so much for the offer — I'm really excited about [specific thing].
I did want to discuss the compensation package.
Based on my research and conversations with others in the field,
I was hoping we could get to [specific number or range].
Is there flexibility there?"
```
